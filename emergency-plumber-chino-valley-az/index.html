Season first

Cold nights in Chino Valley and what they do to pipes

Chino Valley sits near 4,750 feet on State Route 89. The mean January low is about 21.5 degrees Fahrenheit and annual precipitation is about 11.5 inches. Those are the town-area normals, and hard-freeze nights come every winter.

For a homeowner, that means exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces, well houses and outdoor hose bibs are the first to fail. Insulate them, disconnect hoses in fall and keep one faucet dripping during a hard freeze.

Town background

Where the town came from, and why it matters to your pipes

Chino Valley was incorporated in 1970 and counted 13,020 residents in 2020. The area dates to Camp Whipple at Del Rio Springs in December 1863, the first territorial offices, and later to ranching and a railroad branch to Jerome in 1895.

Because the area has farming and ranching roots, many parcels can include outbuildings, well houses and long service lines that are older than the house. When we arrive, we check those outbuildings and the lines running to them, not just the kitchen.

Water

Water supply in town

The Town of Chino Valley Utilities Division delivers drinking water, and the office is on Rodeo Drive. If you are on town water, your service line starts at the meter and ends at the house. If you are on a private well, the pressure tank and pump are part of your system.

Ask the Town for its annual water quality report if you are on town water, or test your tap before choosing a softener. Our water softener and filtration service starts with that test.
