Placement

Where the heater goes matters

A water heater needs air, clearance and a drain path. Gas units need combustion air and a flue that rises continuously to the roof. Electric units need clearance for service and a circuit sized to the heater.

We also look at where a leak would go. A pan with a drain line to a safe spot protects drywall and subfloor if the tank ever fails, and that is an easy detail to add during a new install.

Local climate

Garage and exterior closet installs in a cold-nights town

Prescott Valley averages a January low of roughly 26 degrees Fahrenheit at about 5,100 feet. Many homes put the heater in a garage or an exterior closet. Pipes on exterior walls and in unheated garages need insulation to hold temperature and resist freezing.

We insulate exposed hot and cold lines near the heater and recommend a heater location that stays out of the worst cold when the layout allows.

Scope

What a clean install includes

  • New water connectors and shutoff valves at the heater
  • Temperature and pressure relief valve with a proper discharge pipe
  • Gas line connection tested for leaks, or a properly sized electric circuit
  • Venting set at a continuous rise for gas units
  • Thermal expansion tank where the plumbing is a closed system
  • Final test: purge, leak check, temperature set and a walk-through

Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Prescott Valley building department whether your project needs one. We tell you what to expect before scheduling.

Codes and standards

New heaters should be installed according to the maker instructions and the locally adopted plumbing code, whether that is the UPC or the IPC. Gas appliances are tested to ANSI Z21 standards, and the installation manual sets clearances and venting.
