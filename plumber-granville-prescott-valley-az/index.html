The area

Where Granville sits and what the homes are like

Real-estate guides place Granville on the western side of Prescott Valley, north of State Route 69 and off Glassford Hill Road. The same guides describe a mix of single-family homes and townhomes, mostly single-story, ranging from ranch to more modern styles. They also describe the housing as primarily under 20 years old, with building continuing through recent years.

We do not have a boundary map for the community, so we describe it by those anchor roads and not by exact lot lines. If you are not sure whether your address is in Granville, tell us the street and we will talk it through on the phone.

Local guides also list two clubhouses with pools and fitness rooms, plus parks and a dog park within the community. For a plumber, that mostly means a lot of homes on similar systems, which makes it easy to compare notes when you call.

Practical guidance

Plumbing questions that suit newer homes

A home built in the last two decades is not immune to plumbing trouble, but the questions are different from those in a 1970s house. Appliances and fixtures are often young enough that a manufacturer or builder warranty may still apply, and the first step is to check before paying for a repair.

We do not assume anything about the pipes inside a particular Granville home. What we can say is that newer construction commonly uses modern supply materials, and that any home benefits from a working shutoff and a known water pressure.

Water heater age and warranty

Find the date on the tank label. If the heater is still under warranty, call the maker before you pay for parts. Water heater repair is the usual next step when it is not.

Slow leaks at fixtures

A faucet drip or a running toilet is cheap to fix early. Toilet repair and faucet repair are two of the most common small calls.

Hidden moisture

A damp wall, a warm spot on the floor or a bill that jumps with no change in use can point to a hidden leak. Leak detection finds it without opening every wall.

Slow or gurgling drains

A single slow sink is usually local. Several fixtures slowing at once points to the main line, and drain cleaning is the first thing to try.

Seasonal note

Cold nights and your pipes

Prescott Valley sits at roughly 5,100 feet, and December, January and February average lows run from about 25 to 28 degrees Fahrenheit. Outdoor spigots and any pipe in an unheated garage or exterior wall can freeze on a clear winter night. Disconnect hoses in the fall, keep the garage door closed on freezing nights and know where your main shutoff is before you need it.

1

Find the main shutoff

Look near where the water line enters the house, often in the garage or a utility closet. Turn it clockwise to close.

2

Stop the source

If one fixture is leaking, close its small shutoff valve under the sink or behind the toilet first.

3

Protect the floor

Move rugs and boxes, lay towels and shut off power to any outlet that water has reached.

4

Take a photo

A quick photo of the leak helps when you call and when you talk to your insurer.
