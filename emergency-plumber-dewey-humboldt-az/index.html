Housing first

The housing and the lots we see in Dewey-Humboldt

The town counted 4,326 residents in the 2020 census across about 18.8 square miles. That works out to far fewer homes per acre than in Prescott Valley, and it shapes the plumbing. Lots tend to be larger, service lines can run farther from the street and some properties may use septic systems instead of a sewer connection.

The practical result is that a leak on a long supply line can run unnoticed for days, and a sewer problem may be a septic problem. We ask which you have on the first call, because it decides who to send and what to bring.

History with a point

Dewey, Humboldt and the mining past

Dewey began as a stage station called Aqua Fria in 1872 and was renamed Dewey in 1898. Humboldt grew around its smelter. The town was renamed from Val Verde on November 7, 1905, and a new smelter with a daily capacity of 1,000 tons opened in 1906. Dewey and Humboldt merged when the town incorporated on December 20, 2004.

That history matters to a plumber for two reasons. Older homes and outbuildings anywhere can carry aging galvanized or copper plumbing, so we check for corroded pipes and shutoff valves that no longer close. And the Town lists the Iron King Mine and Smelter as a federal Superfund-related site. If your home runs on a private well, ask the county or state health department about testing before you size a filter.

Before We Arrive

What to do before we arrive

1

Find the main shutoff

On rural lots it is often at the meter box near the road or on the pipe where it enters the house. Close it clockwise.

2

Cut power near water

If water is near outlets or the panel, switch off those circuits only if you can reach the breaker safely.

3

Clear the area

Move rugs, electronics and stored items out of the wet zone and keep children and pets away.

4

Call the 24/7 line

Tell the dispatcher your street, whether you have a well or town water and whether the drains are sewer or septic.

Top calls

Services Dewey-Humboldt homeowners call us for most

Drain and sewer backups lead the list. Drain cleaning clears sinks, tubs and the line to the septic tank or sewer, and hydro jetting cleans heavy buildup. Leaks follow, and leak detection is the fastest route to the source on a long supply line. Water heaters round out the list, and water heater repair covers no-hot-water calls.

We also handle water line repair where a service line has failed between the meter and the house, and burst pipe repair for frozen lines on cold nights.
