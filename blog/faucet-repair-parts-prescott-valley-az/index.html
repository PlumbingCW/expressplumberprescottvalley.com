Faucet Repair Parts: What Prescott Valley, AZ Homes Need

Most faucet repairs need one or two small parts. The challenge is picking the right ones. Faucets look alike and the parts inside are not interchangeable between makers or models.

This guide explains the parts that wear out, how to identify your faucet and the one trick that saves a second trip to the store.

Identify it

Start by identifying the faucet type

Look under the sink and at the handle. One handle that lifts and swings usually means a cartridge or ball. Two handles often mean compression or ceramic disc parts. A pull-down sprayer adds a hose and a weight to the list.

The brand name is usually on the handle or the body. A model number may be stamped on the base or printed inside the cabinet.

Shopping tip

The old-part trick

Take the old cartridge or seat with you. Matching by sight beats guessing from a photo. Take a picture of the faucet and a photo of the label on the box if you have it. Many hardware stores also stock a manufacturer-specific parts chart.

If the maker has a parts diagram online, search by model number. It saves time.

Quick fixes

Do the simple things first

Try these before you buy anything

  • ✓Unscrew the aerator and soak it in vinegar to clear scale
  • ✓Tighten a loose handle with the set screw
  • ✓Check the supply line connections for a loose nut
  • ✓Make sure the shutoff valve is fully open
  • ✓Run the faucet for a moment to clear air after any work
Repair

Replace a cartridge

Shut off the water at the fixture and open the faucet to drain it. Remove the handle and any retaining nut or clip, then pull the cartridge straight out. Put the new one in the same orientation, reassemble and test.

If the faucet body is pitted or the finish is worn, replace the whole faucet. Our faucet repair page explains when repair is not worth it.

Outside

Outdoor faucets and hose bibs

Outdoor spigots fail from freezing more than from wear. A bib that drips from the handle needs a packing nut and washer. One that sprays from behind the wall has split inside and needs a plumber.

Finish

Faucet finishes and why they matter

Parts sold for a polished chrome faucet will not always match a brushed nickel or matte black one, since the finish is applied to handles, caps and trim. Buy trim parts in the same finish as your faucet, and expect that an old finish may be discontinued.

Internal parts such as cartridges, O-rings and seats do not show, so their finish does not matter.

Toolbox

Tools worth having

  • Adjustable wrench and a basin wrench for nuts that sit up behind the sink
  • Hex keys for set screws on handles
  • A small flat screwdriver for popping off caps
  • Plumber grease for O-rings
  • A flashlight and a bowl to keep small parts from rolling away

Lay parts out in the order you removed them. A phone photo at each step is the cheapest insurance against a puzzle at the end. If the new cartridge sits higher than the old one, the retaining nut may be a different size, and that is a sign you have the wrong part.

When you have done everything right and the faucet still leaks, the valve body may be worn. That is the point to call a plumber or replace the faucet.

Codes and standards

Faucets are made to ASME A112.18.1, the standard for plumbing supply fittings, and the EPA WaterSense label caps bathroom faucet flow at 1.5 gallons per minute. Look for that label when you replace a faucet.

Prescott Valley note: outdoor bibs and cold snaps

Prescott Valley sits at about 5,100 feet with a January average low of about 26 degrees Fahrenheit. A hose left connected in January traps water in the bib and lets it freeze and split. A frost-free sillcock sits inside a heated wall, but a failed one still leaks when a hose is left on.

Disconnect hoses in the fall and shut off indoor valves to outdoor lines if you have them. If a bib starts leaking inside the wall after a freeze, close the main water and call. Our burst pipe repair covers that situation.

Special cases

Kitchen faucets with sprayers and pull-downs

A kitchen faucet adds a hose, a weight and a spray head. If the spray head leaks, the cause is usually a worn seal or a clogged screen at the head. If water drips from under the faucet when you use the sprayer, the hose connection is the likely source.

Parts for these are specific to the brand. A diverter inside the faucet body switches water between the spout and the sprayer, and a worn diverter makes the stream weak. Replacing it is a short job on most models.

Know the limit

When to stop shopping for parts

Parts chase has a cost too. If you have made two trips to the store and the faucet still drips, the valve body may be worn, or you may have the wrong model. A plumber can identify the faucet on sight, bring the right cartridge and finish in one visit. That is often cheaper than a third trip and another afternoon of work.

FAQs

Faucet parts questions for Prescott Valley homes

Find the brand and model, or take the old cartridge to the store. Cartridges differ in length, stem shape and spline count.

Mineral buildup and sediment collect in the screen. Soak it in vinegar and rinse. Frequent clogging can signal hard water or debris from pipes.

If parts are available and the finish is good, repair. Replace the faucet if it is corroded, leaks at the body or has no parts available.

Usually the fixture shutoff valves under the sink are enough. If they do not close fully, use the main shutoff.

Many braided lines last several years. Replace them when you see rust, kinks or any leaks at the nuts.
