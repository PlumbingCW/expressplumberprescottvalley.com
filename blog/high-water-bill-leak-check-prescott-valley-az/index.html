High Water Bill? How to Check for a Leak in Prescott Valley, AZ

A sudden jump in your water bill usually has a cause you can find. Before you call anyone, run a quick test with your water meter. If the meter moves when every fixture is off, water is going somewhere you cannot see.

This guide walks through the meter test, the toilet dye test and the places where hidden leaks usually start.

Step one

The two-minute meter test

  1. Turn off every faucet and appliance that uses water, including ice makers and irrigation.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in a box near the street, and lift the lid.
  3. Look at the small leak indicator, often a triangle or a small dial that spins when water is flowing.
  4. Write down the reading, wait fifteen to thirty minutes and check again.
  5. If the numbers moved or the indicator spins, water is escaping.

Meter faces vary, so ask your utility how to read yours. Do not touch the utility valve, which belongs to the utility.

Step two

The toilet dye test

Toilets cause most silent leaks. Add a few drops of food coloring to the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and look at the bowl. If color appears, the flapper is leaking.

A flapper leak can waste many gallons a day without making a sound. Replacing a flapper is a low-cost fix. Our guide to the toilet repair service covers what else to look at.

Next step

If the meter keeps moving

Close the main shutoff to the house and watch the meter. If it stops moving, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the house, in the service line. Our water line repair page explains that case.

A professional leak detection visit uses listening and thermal tools to find the leak without opening walls. Leak detection explains the process.

Track it

How to track your usage over a week

If the meter test finds nothing, start a simple log. Read the meter at the same time each morning and each evening for a week. Write down the numbers and note what ran during the day, such as laundry, irrigation or guests.

A leak shows up as a steady rise at night or while the house is empty. Normal use rises in the morning and evening and goes flat when nobody is home.

Call them

Questions for your utility

  • Ask for your usage history by month and by billing cycle
  • Ask whether your meter was read or estimated
  • Ask what units your meter reports
  • Ask whether any leak adjustment program exists
  • Ask whether the meter can be tested for accuracy

A plumber is the right call when the meter moves with everything off, when you find damp spots or when you hear running water. Our Chino Valley page covers calls from well and service line owners there.

Codes and standards

According to the EPA WaterSense program, the average household wastes nearly 10,000 gallons of water a year to leaks, and ten percent of homes waste 90 gallons a day or more. A meter test is the fastest way to know if you are one of them.

Prescott Valley note: irrigation season

Prescott Valley averages about 15 inches of precipitation a year, so lawns and landscaping rely on irrigation for much of the year. A stuck sprinkler valve or a broken head can run for hours without anyone noticing. Check irrigation zones first when a bill jumps in summer.

Your bill comes from the Town or its water provider. Ask the Town utility billing office for your usage history by month. A sudden step up from a steady baseline points to a leak, while a seasonal rise points to irrigation.

What we see

Common leaks in newer homes

Homes that went up during Prescott Valley growth years are mostly newer, and their typical leaks are small ones. A failed toilet flapper, a weeping water heater fitting, a stuck irrigation valve or a failed pressure regulator account for many high bills.

A pressure regulator that has failed lets full street pressure into the house. That can make small leaks worse and it is worth testing if your fixtures sound noisy or your hoses bulge.

Check the pressure with a simple gauge on a hose bib. If it reads well above what is normal for homes, ask a plumber about the regulator.

Next steps

Fixing what you find

If the dye test shows a leaking flapper, replace it and check the meter again. If the irrigation test shows a stuck valve, call the irrigation contractor or fix the valve. If the meter still moves after those checks, the leak is probably in a supply line, and a plumber with listening and thermal tools can find it without cutting walls. Keep your meter readings, because they help the plumber and support an insurance claim.

If your bill stays high even after every fix, ask the utility to test the meter. Meters can drift with age, and a test settles the question with data instead of guesses.
