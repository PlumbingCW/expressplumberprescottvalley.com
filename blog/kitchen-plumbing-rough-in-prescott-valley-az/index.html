Kitchen Plumbing Rough In: A Prescott Valley, AZ Homeowner Guide

A kitchen plumbing rough in is the stage where supply and drain lines are installed inside the walls and floor, before cabinets and finishes go in. It sets where the sink, dishwasher, disposal and ice maker connect. Getting it right saves money, since moving a pipe after the walls close is expensive.

This guide explains what a rough in includes and the decisions to make before the plumber starts.

The scope

What a kitchen rough in includes

  • Hot and cold supply lines to the sink
  • A drain with a trap arm and a vent
  • A dishwasher supply and drain connection
  • A power or switch location for a garbage disposal
  • An ice maker or refrigerator water line
  • Shutoff valves for each fixture

The plumber positions each line to match your cabinet plan. If your plan changes after the rough in, lines may need to be moved.

Planning

Decide these before the plumber arrives

Settle these items first

  • ✓The sink model and its rough-in sheet from the manufacturer
  • ✓Whether you want one bowl, two bowls or a second prep sink
  • ✓A disposal or not, and the model
  • ✓Dishwasher model and its drain requirements
  • ✓Pot filler or a filtered water faucet
  • ✓Island sink location if you are adding one
Drain

The drain and vent

A sink drain needs a slope to carry water away and a vent so it drains smoothly. An island sink is harder, because the vent cannot run up through a countertop. Plumbers use a loop vent or another approved method, and the code requirements depend on the local adopted code.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Prescott Valley building department what your project needs. We tell you what to expect on inspection day.

Appliances

Dishwasher and disposal connections

A dishwasher drains through a hose that loops high under the counter before it connects to the disposal inlet or the drain. This loop helps stop sink water from flowing back into the dishwasher. A disposal needs a clear connection, an electrical source and room under the sink.

If you want a new disposal, ask your plumber to plan the connections so the unit can be swapped later without opening the wall. Our garbage disposal repair page covers the types and common issues.

Common errors

Mistakes that cost money after the walls close

  • Rough-in set for a sink that is later changed to a different size or depth
  • Shutoff valves buried behind a finished wall with no access panel
  • A drain trap arm that is too long and slopes the wrong way
  • No power planned for a disposal or an instant hot water tap
  • Ice maker line ending where the refrigerator will not sit

Every one of these is easy to avoid when the walls are open, and costly after drywall and tile. Bring your final appliance and fixture models to the plumber, along with the cabinet plan, before rough in starts.

The best time to add a second sink, a filtered tap or a pot filler is now. Adding them later means cutting finished walls and floors.

Final check

Walk-through before the walls close

Walk the rough in with your plumber

  • ✓Check the sink drain position against the cabinet base
  • ✓Confirm shutoff valves are accessible and labelled
  • ✓Check the dishwasher supply and drain stubs against the appliance specs
  • ✓Confirm the inspection is scheduled before drywall
  • ✓Take photos of every wall for future reference

Those photos are valuable. Years later, you will know exactly where pipes run before you hang a cabinet or cut into a wall. We also work on kitchen projects in Dewey-Humboldt.

Codes and standards

A rough in is inspected under the locally adopted plumbing code, either the UPC or the IPC, and the dishwasher drain should follow the maker manual and code rules for a high loop or an air gap.

Prescott Valley note: remodels in a fast-growing town

Prescott Valley went from 8,858 residents in 1990 to 38,822 in 2010 and 46,785 in 2020, so many kitchens are now old enough to remodel. When walls open in a house from that era, check the supply lines and shutoff valves while the plumber is there. It is the cheapest time to replace them.

The Town building department decides what permits apply to your remodel. Call before you order fixtures, and your plumber can adjust the plan to match what the Town asks for.

Budgeting

Costs and who does what

Rough-in cost depends on how many fixtures you have, how far they are from the existing lines and how much of the wall and floor is open. Moving a sink to the other side of the kitchen costs more than keeping it where it is.

Ask the plumber what is included. Does the price cover the fixtures, the permit, the inspection and the final connection? A clear scope avoids a change order after the walls close.

Scheduling

Coordinating with the other trades

Plumbing rough in sits between framing, electrical and drywall. If the electrician needs a circuit for the disposal or a dishwasher outlet, plan it at the same time. If the cabinet maker changes the sink base, tell the plumber before the walls close. A short meeting on site with every trade saves days of rework and avoids pipes that land where a cabinet needs to be.
