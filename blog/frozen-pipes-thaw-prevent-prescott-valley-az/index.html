Frozen Pipes: How to Thaw and Prevent Them in Prescott Valley, AZ

If a faucet gives you a trickle or nothing on a freezing morning, a pipe may be frozen. Act quickly but carefully. Open the faucet, find the frozen section and warm it gently. Never use an open flame.

This guide covers how to thaw a pipe safely, how to tell when it has burst and how to prevent a repeat on the next cold night.

Step by step

How to thaw a frozen pipe

1

Open the faucet

Leave it open so melting ice and water can flow out and relieve pressure.

2

Find the frozen spot

Check pipes along exterior walls, in garages, crawlspaces and attics. A frosted or bulging pipe is the likely spot.

3

Warm it gently

Use a hair dryer, a heating pad or warm towels. Start at the faucet end and work toward the frozen section.

4

Check for leaks

When water flows, look for drips along the pipe. A split may show only after the thaw.

Hazards

What never to do

Avoid these

  • ✓Never use an open flame, torch or propane heater on a pipe
  • ✓Do not use a kerosene or charcoal heater indoors
  • ✓Do not leave a heat source unattended
  • ✓Do not hammer or force a frozen valve
  • ✓Do not ignore a pipe that bulges or cracks, since it will leak when it thaws
Diagnosis

Burst or just frozen

A split pipe leaks when the ice melts. If you see water spraying, a wet ceiling or a stain growing on a wall, close the main shutoff and call a plumber. Open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line.

Our burst pipe repair page explains what the repair involves, and the 24/7 line takes calls on cold nights.

Prevent it

Prevention before the next freeze

  • Insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces and attics
  • Disconnect and store garden hoses and shut off outdoor lines
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls
  • Let a faucet drip on hard-freeze nights
  • Seal gaps around pipes where they enter the house
  • Keep the heat on if you travel, set to a safe temperature

Travel plan

What to do if you will be away in winter

An empty house is the riskiest case, since nobody notices a burst until the damage is done. If you leave for several days in cold weather, keep the heat on at a safe temperature, open cabinet doors on exterior walls and ask a neighbor to check the house.

For a long absence, you can close the main shutoff and drain the lines. Ask a plumber how to do it safely for your system, especially if you have a water heater and a fire sprinkler system.

High-risk spots

Where pipes freeze first

Exterior walls

Pipes against a cold wall with little insulation lose heat fastest.

Unheated garages

A garage can be as cold as the outdoors on a still night.

Crawlspaces

Vents let cold air blow across pipes under the floor.

Outdoor bibs and irrigation backflow devices

Water sits in exposed fittings and freezes first.

A leak after the thaw is common. Walk the house when the pipes warm up and check ceilings, walls and cabinets for water. If you see any, close the main and call us.

Heat tape on a pipe can help in a trouble spot, but follow the maker instructions, since misused tape can start a fire. For calls in nearby Dewey-Humboldt, our 24/7 line is open too.

Codes and standards

Model plumbing codes such as the IPC call for water pipes to be protected from freezing when they run in exterior walls, attics or crawlspaces. Heat tape should be listed by a testing lab such as UL and installed according to the maker instructions.

Prescott Valley and Chino Valley note: how cold it gets

In Prescott Valley, 1991 to 2020 normals show average lows near 25 degrees Fahrenheit in December and about 26 in January. The town sits at about 5,100 feet. Chino Valley is a little lower in elevation but colder, with a mean January low of about 21.5 degrees.

Those are averages. Individual nights are colder, and wind makes pipes on exterior walls freeze sooner. Treat any forecast near or below the low 20s as a freeze night and take the prevention steps above. We serve Chino Valley and Dewey-Humboldt too.

Before the first freeze

A short winter plumbing checklist

Do these in the fall

  • ✓Locate and label the main water shutoff and show everyone in the house
  • ✓Disconnect garden hoses and cover or drain outdoor bibs
  • ✓Insulate pipes in the garage, crawlspace and attic
  • ✓Seal air gaps where pipes enter the house
  • ✓Service the heating system so it does not fail on a cold night
  • ✓Keep the number of a 24/7 plumber where you can find it

Fifteen minutes in October can save a very bad morning in January. The steps above cost little, and they are the ones plumbers recommend most often.

Insurance

After a burst: first steps for your claim

Take photos and video of the burst pipe, the water and every damaged item before you clean up. Keep receipts for emergency repairs and for drying equipment. Call your insurer promptly, since policies set time limits on reporting. Ask what the policy expects you to do to prevent further damage, and keep the old pipe section if the adjuster asks to see it.
