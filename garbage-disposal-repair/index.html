Disposal failure: what to do first

Safe first steps

Jams

Why a disposal hums but will not spin

A hum means the motor has power but the blades are stuck. A bone, a fruit pit or a utensil is the usual cause. Rotating the blades with a hex key through the bottom socket can free it.

If the motor trips the reset button again and again, the motor may be worn out and the unit needs to be replaced.

Leaks

Leaks from the disposal

Water under the sink might come from the sink flange, the dishwasher hose, the drain connection or the housing itself. A leak from the body of the disposal means the seal has failed and the unit needs to be replaced. A leak from a connection is usually a gasket or a nut.

We run water and trace the leak to its source so you do not pay for the wrong part.

Install

Replacing a disposal

Most homes use a one-half or three-quarter horsepower unit. Heavier use or a household that cooks often benefits from a larger motor and a sturdier grinding stage. We remove the old unit, set the new flange and mount, reconnect the drain and dishwasher line and test the flow.

A new disposal needs a switch or a power source. We check the wiring and add an outlet or switch where the setup allows.

Codes and standards

Household waste disposers are tested to UL 430. Plumbing codes such as the UPC and IPC also set rules for the trap and the dishwasher drain connection, including a high loop or an air gap.
