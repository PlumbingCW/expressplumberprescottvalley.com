Urgent steps

Active leak: what to do right now

Water that is visibly running through a ceiling or a wall is an emergency. It can soak insulation, bring down drywall and reach electrical wiring in minutes.

Shut off the main water valve, switch off power to affected rooms if it is safe to reach the panel, move valuables and call us. If you cannot find the valve, we can talk you through finding it.

Meter test

Meter still running or a high water bill

Many leak calls begin with a bill that does not match your usage. The quickest check is the meter. Turn off every fixture and appliance, then watch the small dial or flow indicator on the meter. If it moves, water is going somewhere.

We then isolate the house line, the irrigation, the water heater and the fixtures one by one. That narrows the leak to a zone before we use any equipment.

Interior

Ceiling and wall leaks

A stain that spreads across a ceiling points to a supply line, a drain or a roof. Moisture readings and thermal imaging help separate them. A supply leak stays active when the house is dry outside, and a roof leak appears with rain.

Underneath

Under the house and crawlspace

A leak below a raised floor can wet insulation and encourage mold. We inspect accessible crawlspaces for dripping lines, damp soil and sagging insulation, then repair the line or advise on further drying.

Outdoors

Buried lines, irrigation and meter-box leaks

A leak in the yard may show as a soft patch of ground, a lush strip of grass or a meter box that is always full. Irrigation lines and the service line from the meter are the usual sources. We pressure-test each to find which one is losing water.

Pool and spa leaks are a separate specialty. We check the supply and return plumbing, and if the pool shell is losing water, we tell you plainly and point you to a pool specialist.

Codes and standards
