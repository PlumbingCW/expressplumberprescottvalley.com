Urgent steps

Sewer line failure: what to do first

A failing sewer line can leak wastewater into the soil under your yard or foundation. That is a health and property problem, not just a plumbing annoyance.

Stop using water, avoid walking through wet ground over the line and call us. If sewage is inside, keep people out of the area and call a restoration crew once the plumber has stopped the source.

Diagnosis

How we find the break

A camera on a flexible cable runs through the line and records its path. We note the depth and the location of each defect, and a locator at the surface marks the exact spot.

Common findings are cracked clay joints, roots entering at a coupling, a bellied section holding water and a separated joint where soil has shifted.

Dig and replace

  • Replaces the failed section with new pipe
  • Best when a long run is badly damaged
  • Needs excavation and restoration
  • Longest-lasting for heavy damage

Pipe lining and spot repair

  • A resin liner or short repair inside the existing pipe
  • Less digging, less yard damage
  • Suited to cracks and root entry
  • Not a fit for a fully collapsed pipe
Trenchless option

Pipe lining and spot repair

Trenchless lining pulls a resin-saturated tube through the old pipe and cures it into a new pipe inside the old one. It works best when the pipe is intact enough to carry the liner. A collapsed section or a severe belly usually needs excavation.

A spot repair replaces only the damaged section after a small excavation. We recommend it when the camera shows one defect on an otherwise healthy line.

Local note

Older lines in the area

Prescott Valley is largely recent construction. Older homes and rural lots near Dewey-Humboldt and Chino Valley can have clay or cast iron sewer lines that fail from age and root pressure. We scope each property individually, and a camera shows what material you have.

Permit rules vary, so ask your town or the county about permit needs for sewer work, and the plumber will tell you what to expect.

Codes and standards

Trenchless liners are manufactured and installed to ASTM standards such as ASTM F1216 for inversion lining. Ask any contractor which standard its liner follows. Permit rules vary, so ask your town or county what applies to sewer work.
