Urgent steps

A leaking water line: what to do first

A line leaking under the yard or driveway wastes water and can undermine pavement. Close the shutoff valve at the house, or the one at the meter if you know where it is. Leave the meter itself alone, because that is utility equipment.

Then call us. We will confirm where the leak is and explain whether a spot repair or a replacement makes sense.

Symptoms

Signs your service line is failing

Look for these

  • ✓A wet or sunken patch in the yard that stays after the sprinklers are off
  • ✓A water meter that moves with every fixture off
  • ✓A sudden drop in water pressure at every tap
  • ✓Discolored water after work on the line
  • ✓A water bill that rises without more usage
Options

Repair or replace the line

Spot repair

  • Fixes one leak at a joint or small break
  • Smaller excavation
  • Lower upfront cost
  • Does not fix an aging line

Line replacement

  • Replaces the pipe from the meter to the house
  • Trenchless methods can limit yard damage
  • Better when the pipe is old or leaking repeatedly
  • Higher upfront cost, longer life

Trenchless methods such as pipe pulling can replace a line with only two small access pits. They are not a fit for every site, and we tell you whether your yard and layout allow it.

Pressure

Low water pressure

Low pressure at every tap suggests a service line problem, a partly closed valve or a failing pressure regulator. Low pressure at only one fixture is a fixture problem. We test pressure at a hose bib to separate the two.

High pressure can be just as damaging. A regulator that has failed lets full street pressure into the house, which stresses fittings and fixtures.

Who owns which part of the line varies. The utility typically owns equipment up to the meter, and the property owner owns the line from the meter to the house. Ask your water provider to confirm for your address before work starts.

Codes and standards

Underground utility locates are standard practice through 811, the national call-before-you-dig number. Drinking water pipe, including polyethylene pipe made to AWWA C901, should be certified to NSF/ANSI 61.
