Tankless Water Heater Installation in Prescott Valley, AZ

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, so the hot water does not run out in a long shower. It also needs the right gas supply or electrical service to work, and that is where most tankless projects succeed or fail.

Fit

Is tankless right for your house

A tankless heater is rated by how many gallons per minute it can raise by a set temperature. In the cold months, groundwater is colder, so the same heater delivers less hot water than in summer. We size the unit for winter, not July.

Gas units often need a larger gas line than a tank heater did, plus a new vent. Electric units can need a heavy circuit that an older panel may not have. We check both before quoting.

Tank heater

  • Lower upfront cost
  • Stored hot water can run out
  • Takes floor space
  • Standby heat loss all day

Tankless heater

  • Hot water as long as the flow is within capacity
  • Wall-mounted and compact
  • No standing tank to fail and flood
  • Needs gas line or electrical upgrades, and annual care
Maintenance

Flush and descale service

Mineral scale builds inside the heat exchanger. Left alone, it cuts flow and triggers error codes. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit and clears that scale.

Many makers call for a flush about once a year, and homes with harder water may need it more often. Ask your water utility for its annual water quality report, or test your tap, to see how hard your supply is. We flush units whether we installed them or not.

Signs a tankless unit needs attention

  • ✓Water that runs warm but never hot at a full flow
  • ✓An error code on the display that returns after a reset
  • ✓Temperature swings in a steady shower
  • ✓Reduced flow at fixtures on the hot side only
  • ✓Noise from the unit during heating
Repair

Repair and the 110 volt question

Common repairs include replacing a flow sensor, an igniter, a thermistor or a clogged inlet screen. Some small point-of-use units run on a standard 120 volt plug, and they are sized for one sink or a small appliance, not a whole house. Whole-house electric tankless units generally need heavy 240 volt circuits.

We tell you plainly whether a unit you are considering will cover the whole house or only a single tap.

Codes and standards

Gas tankless heaters are listed to ANSI Z21 standards and the installation manual sets the gas line size and the venting. According to most makers, descaling at the interval in the manual is a condition of the warranty, so keep a record of each flush.
