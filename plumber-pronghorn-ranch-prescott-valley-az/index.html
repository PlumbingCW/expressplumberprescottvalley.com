Practical guidance

Bigger lots, more outdoor plumbing

A quarter-acre lot or larger often has outdoor spigots at the front and back, a sprinkler or drip system and sometimes a hose bib near a shed or garden. Each is a place where water can leak or freeze.

Prescott Valley winter lows average about 25 to 28 degrees from December through February, so an exposed spigot can freeze on a clear night. A frozen pipe can split without showing anything until it thaws, which is why the first warm morning is when leaks appear.

Spigots and hose bibs

Disconnect hoses, drain the line and cover the bib before a hard freeze. Faucet repair fixes the fixture itself.

Frozen or split pipes

If a pipe has frozen, shut off the water at the main before it thaws and call about burst pipe repair.

Supply line leaks

A wet area in the yard with no rain points to a leak on the line. Water line repair covers the run from meter to house.

Everyday fixtures

Running toilets and slow sinks are common calls. Toilet repair and garbage disposal repair are quick visits.

Freeze steps

If a pipe freezes or splits

1

Close the main

Turn off the water at the main shutoff so a split pipe cannot flood when it thaws.

2

Open a tap

Open a faucet near the frozen section to relieve pressure and let water out as it melts.

3

Warm the area gently

Use warm air, not a flame. Never use a torch on a frozen pipe.

4

Check for leaks

Once water flows, look along the pipe for wet spots and call if you find one.

Before winter, walk your lot

  • ✓Disconnect every hose and drain the spigot.
  • ✓Check that any shutoff for an outdoor line closes fully.
  • ✓Look for drips at the meter box and around the foundation.
  • ✓Know where the main shutoff is and make sure everyone at home can reach it.
