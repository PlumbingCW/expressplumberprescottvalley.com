Urgent replacement

When a dead tank is an emergency

A tank that is leaking from the shell is on borrowed time. Steel tanks corrode from the inside, and once the shell is breached there is no patch. The longer the leak runs, the more subfloor and drywall get wet.

Treat these as urgent: water at the base of the tank, rust-colored hot water, a tank that has stopped heating for a household that cannot wait, and a relief valve that discharges on its own.

Sizing

How we pick the right size

Size depends on how many people shower, run the dishwasher and do laundry in the same window. A forty gallon tank suits one or two people. A fifty gallon tank is the common choice for families of three or four. Larger households or a soaking tub usually call for a bigger tank or a faster recovery rate.

We also check the first hour rating, which tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in a busy hour. That number matters more than tank size alone.

Keeping an old tank running

  • Repairs repeat as the shell ages
  • A sudden leak can flood a garage or closet
  • Higher energy use as sediment builds
  • No warranty on the old unit

Replacing it now

  • A new tank carries a manufacturer warranty
  • The swap is planned instead of an emergency
  • Fresh anode rod and clean tank floor
  • Code-current relief valve and discharge pipe
Process

What happens during the swap

1

Shut off and drain

We close the water and fuel, then drain the old tank to a safe point.

2

Disconnect and remove

The old unit comes out and leaves with us.

3

Set and connect

The new tank is set level in a pan where the space allows, with new connectors and a relief valve.

4

Test and explain

We purge air, check for leaks, test combustion on gas units and set the temperature.

On a gas heater we check the flue draft and the gas line for leaks. On an electric unit we confirm the breaker and wiring match the heater. Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Prescott Valley building department whether a permit applies to your swap, and your plumber will explain what is needed.

Codes and standards

Both model plumbing codes, the UPC and the IPC, call for a temperature and pressure relief valve on a storage water heater, with a discharge pipe routed to a safe spot. Permit rules vary, so ask the Town of Prescott Valley building department what applies to your swap.
