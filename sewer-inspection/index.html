Sewer Camera Inspection in Prescott Valley, AZ

A sewer camera inspection turns a guess into a picture. We feed a waterproof camera through the line from a cleanout, watch the video live and mark any defect with a depth and a location.

It is a good step before buying a home, after a repeat clog or before any sewer repair. You get an explanation, and we can share the video.

What you see

What a camera finds

The camera shows pipe material, joint condition, root intrusion, scale, standing water and cracks. With a locator at the surface, we mark the spot above the defect so you can see how deep and how far from the house it is.

A clean line gets a clean report. A damaged line gets a clear explanation of what is wrong and which repair fits.

Good reasons

When to schedule an inspection

Times a camera inspection pays off

  • ✓You are buying a home and want to see the sewer line before closing
  • ✓A drain clogs again soon after cleaning
  • ✓You smell sewer gas outdoors or notice a wet patch in the yard
  • ✓You are planning landscaping or a new structure over the line
  • ✓You want proof of the line condition for an insurer or a repair estimate

Pre-purchase

Buying a home in Prescott Valley

A home inspection usually looks at plumbing fixtures and visible pipes, not at the buried sewer line. A camera inspection checks the section between the house and the connection point. If the camera finds problems, you can negotiate repairs before closing.

Properties on septic systems, which are common in rural parts of Dewey-Humboldt and Chino Valley, need a separate septic inspection, and we can tell you what to ask for.

Review

Reading the video together

We pause the video at each defect and tell you what it is. A cracked joint, a root intrusion and a belly are different problems with different fixes. You get the footage, a depth and a distance from the cleanout for each finding.

If the line is healthy, you can plan maintenance with confidence. If it is damaged, you can get a repair quote from us or compare it with another plumber using the same video.

What a good inspection report includes

  • ✓The video recording of the full run
  • ✓Distance from the cleanout to each defect
  • ✓Depth at the surface locator mark
  • ✓Pipe material and diameter where visible
  • ✓A plain-language list of recommended next steps

Codes and standards

Camera findings are commonly coded using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program, the industry reference for describing cracks, roots and joint defects. We explain each finding in plain words, with a depth and a distance.
