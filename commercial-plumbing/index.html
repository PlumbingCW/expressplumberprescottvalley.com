Leaks

Leak detection for a business

Commercial leak calls can involve long runs of pipe over several tenants. We start with meter and zone testing to narrow the area, then locate the leak with listening and thermal tools. We tell you honestly when a leak is beyond a small commercial job, and point you to the right specialist.

Planned maintenance

Preventive visits that protect a business

Many commercial emergencies are predictable. Kitchen drains clog on a schedule, water heaters age, shutoff valves seize from lack of use. A scheduled visit lets a plumber exercise valves, check pressure, look at the water heater and clear lines before a busy weekend.

For a restaurant, a regular cleaning of kitchen drains costs far less than a backup during a dinner rush. For an office, a yearly look at restroom fixtures and shutoff valves catches small leaks before they stain ceilings.

A simple preventive checklist for a small business

  • ✓Find and label the main water shutoff and each branch valve
  • ✓Test shutoff valves twice a year so they do not seize
  • ✓Check restroom flush valves and faucets for slow leaks
  • ✓Keep grease out of kitchen drains and clean the traps
  • ✓Look at the water heater for rust, leaks and the relief valve discharge
  • ✓Keep a written list of whom to call after hours

Codes and standards

Commercial work follows the locally adopted plumbing code, either the UPC or the IPC, and it can add grease interceptor and backflow rules that differ by jurisdiction. Ask your town building department what applies to your property.
