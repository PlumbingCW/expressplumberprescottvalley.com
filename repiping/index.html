Repiping in Prescott Valley, AZ

Repiping replaces the supply lines inside a house. It is the right step when leaks keep returning, pipes are galvanized and corroded or low pressure and rusty water point to old steel.

A planned repipe is far cheaper than a series of emergency repairs and the water damage that comes with them. We explain how we would approach your home before any wall is opened.

When to repipe

Signs your pipes are due

Common reasons we recommend a repipe

  • ✓Leaks that return after repairs, in different spots
  • ✓Galvanized steel pipe with rust-brown water and low pressure
  • ✓Pinhole leaks in copper along several runs
  • ✓Old polybutylene supply lines
  • ✓A remodel that opens walls anyway
Materials

PEX or copper

MaterialStrengthsThings to weigh
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, quicker install, resists splitting in a freeze better than rigid pipeNot for outdoor sunlight exposure, needs proper support
CopperLong record, handles heat, familiar to inspectorsMore joints, higher material cost, can pinhole in some water
CPVCLower costCan turn brittle with age and heat

For most homes we recommend PEX, because it runs through walls with fewer cuts and tolerates a cold snap better than rigid pipe. We choose copper where a customer prefers it or where a run is exposed and needs a rigid material.

Process

How a repipe is planned

1

Survey the home

We map fixtures, shutoffs and existing pipe and look at access in attics and crawlspaces.

2

Plan the routes

New lines go through attic, crawlspace or walls with as few openings as possible.

3

Stage the work

We keep water on where we can and work room by room.

4

Patch and test

Every line is pressure-tested, and we patch access holes for painting.

Scope

Whole house or partial

A partial repipe replaces one run, such as the line to a bathroom or the hot side of the house. It makes sense when the rest of the plumbing is sound. A whole-house repipe makes sense when galvanized steel, polybutylene or heavily pinholed copper runs through the home.

Prescott Valley homes mostly date from after 1990, based on census growth, so many will have copper or PEX already. Older homes in Dewey-Humboldt and Chino Valley can still have galvanized steel, and a quick pipe inspection tells you which you have.

What to expect

Living in the house during a repipe

Most homeowners stay in the house during a repipe. The plumber coordinates water shutoffs by area and usually restores water at the end of each day. Plan for dust, noise and short periods without water at specific fixtures.

Move furniture away from walls where access holes will be cut, and protect floors. Pets and children should stay clear of work zones. We explain the schedule before we start so you can plan around it.

Prepare your home

  • ✓Clear closets and cabinets along the planned routes
  • ✓Move valuables and art off walls near the work
  • ✓Keep a supply of drinking water on hand for the short shutoffs
  • ✓Tell the plumber about known problem areas such as old patches or low-pressure fixtures
  • ✓Plan for painting after drywall is patched

Codes and standards

PEX tubing for drinking water is made to ASTM F876 and its fittings to ASTM F877, and potable pipe should carry NSF/ANSI 61 certification. Copper tube follows ASTM B88. We use certified materials and follow the maker instructions.
