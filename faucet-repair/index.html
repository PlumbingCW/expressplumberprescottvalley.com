Urgent signs

Fixture leak: when it cannot wait

A drip can wait for a regular visit. A supply line spraying under a sink or a shutoff valve that will not close cannot. Treat these as emergencies and use the main water shutoff.

Place a bucket under the leak, dry the area and keep power cords clear of the water.

Causes

Why faucets drip

Compression faucets wear at the washer and seat. Cartridge and ball faucets leak at worn seals and O-rings. Ceramic disc faucets fail at the cartridge. Mineral buildup can also keep a faucet from closing.

We identify the type, replace the worn part and test it. If parts are no longer made or the finish is worn, a new faucet may cost less than chasing parts.

Bath

Shower, tub and bathtub faucet repair

A shower valve that drips after you shut it off wastes water and stains the tub. The fix is usually a new cartridge or seat. A tub spout that leaks when the shower runs needs a new diverter.

If you cannot turn a shower valve fully off, the water can feel scalding or cold when someone flushes a toilet. We replace the cartridge and check the mixing valve.

Under the sink

Supply lines and shutoff valves

The braided lines under sinks last a limited time and then burst or leak at the nut. Old shutoff valves can freeze in place or leak at the stem. We replace both so you can shut off a fixture when you need to.

Codes and standards

Faucets are made to ASME A112.18.1, the standard for plumbing supply fittings, and the EPA WaterSense label for bathroom faucets caps flow at 1.5 gallons per minute. Matching parts to the maker model keeps a repair within those specs.
